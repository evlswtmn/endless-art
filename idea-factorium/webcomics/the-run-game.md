# The Run Game

**Status:** `exploring`
**Type:** webcomic
**One-liner:** Two Gen X men discover a magical tome at a farmhouse that grants access to complete alternate universes — then turn it into a competitive creative game with real psychological stakes.

---

## The Concept

THE RUN GAME is a supernatural alternate-universe exploration framework centered on two Gen X men (Gene Sweetman and Greg) who discover a magical tome at a farmhouse, granting access to "Runs" — complete alternate universes designed as psychological growth experiences, creative competitions, and empathetic challenges.

It's not a simulation. Runs are complete universes with full internal logic. The magic system is procedural, not whimsical. The story is a webcomic with three distinct visual styles depending on the type of Run being experienced.

On top of the Runs sits a meta-competitive layer called "The Game" — a callback-detection competition between Gene, Greg, Jason, and Travis that adds stakes, humor, and structural complexity to every Run.

---

## CANON AUTHORITY & PRIORITY RULES

These govern all generation and development. Non-negotiable.

1. **Character continuity overrides plot convenience.** No event may contradict established character psychology, trauma, growth, or visual markers to "move the story faster."
2. **Rules > spectacle.** If a scene is exciting but violates a system rule, the scene is invalid.
3. **Observed reality is binding.** Once something is shown (scar, tattoo, rule, death mechanic, audience voting outcome), it cannot be undone except through explicitly defined multiversal mechanics.
4. **Meta-awareness must escalate slowly.** Characters do not suddenly "get it." Awareness increases through stress, contradiction, and failure.
5. **Crossovers do not overwrite tone.** When universes intersect, both aesthetics coexist in tension — they do not blend cleanly.

## Magic System — Hard Rules

### Magic Time
- A fixed physical location (the farmhouse)
- Exists outside normal causality but anchored to it
- Cannot be moved, destroyed, or replicated
- Always appears mundane when inactive

### Tome Mechanics
- The tome does not create Runs — it indexes access
- Naming a door selects an already-existing universe
- Names must be intentional; sloppy naming increases risk

### Entry & Exit Conditions
Only three exits exist:
1. **Completion** — Run-defined success
2. **Failure** — Run-defined loss
3. **Safe-word activation** — Costs points, leaves psychological residue, cannot be abused repeatedly without consequence

### Time Behavior
- Subjective time ≠ Objective time (time dilation occurs)
- Psychological effects persist regardless of duration
- Aging does NOT carry over physically, but fatigue and trauma do

## The Game (Meta-Layer)

The Game is not just social — it is structural.

### Participants
- **Gene** — Primary player/designer
- **Greg** — Co-protagonist/designer
- **Jason** — Active participant, third-person perspective provider
- **Travis** — Judge ("Basement Court")

### Basement Court
- Travis functions as rule enforcer, not creator
- He interprets rules but does not invent outcomes
- His authority depends on perceived fairness

### Designer Participation Rule
- The designer must go first
- Purpose: prevent sadism, enforce empathy, expose blind spots
- Violations destabilize the Run itself
- Established by Travis as foundational law

### Callback System
- Callbacks are deliberate narrative artifacts embedded by designers
- They function as: psychological tells, Easter eggs, proof of authorship
- **Designer** gains points if callbacks remain undetected
- **Player** gains points if callbacks are caught
- Successful detection earns the "Hero Belt" (imaginary but symbolically significant)
- Detection shifts power dynamics between designer and player

## Run Types (Functional, Not Thematic)

Runs are classified by intended psychological effect, not genre:

1. **Trauma/Horror Runs** — Stress-testing, fear processing, moral injury exposure
   - Dark, desaturated, gritty tones; harsh angular line work; shadow-heavy
2. **Wholesome/Happy Runs** — Repair, bonding, emotional recalibration
   - Pastel tones; soft rounded lines; warm golden-hour lighting
3. **Neutral Deck Runs** — Baseline reality, integration space, perspective correction
   - Natural realistic colors; balanced line work; photorealistic approach
4. **Competitive Runs** — Skill challenge, ego confrontation, designer arrogance punishment risk

## Character Profiles

### GENE SWEETMAN (Protagonist)
- **Age/Generation:** Gen X, early-mid 50s
- **Physical:** Taller, heavier build. Lighter/greyish hair, full beard, rectangular glasses (non-negotiable visual marker), fuller face, kind eyes (core visual constant)
- **Background:** Production operator at factory in Maryland (Eastern Shore, rural area)
- **Psychology:** Empathy-first. Willing to feel. Learns through exposure rather than control. Emotionally open, willing to be vulnerable in Runs
- **First accidental Run:** Medieval fantasy world — "borderline Disney PG grand ole time" (intentionally contrasted with Greg's traumatic experience)
- **Arc Invariant:** Gene never becomes cruel, cynical, or dominant. Confidence increases, empathy remains.
- **Narrative role:** Primary POV for webcomic; represents the player who experiences less trauma due to foreknowledge when swapping Run experiences
- **Continuity markers:** Visual consistency essential; any scars, gear changes, or psychological shifts must be tracked across Runs. Clothing evolves based on Run outcomes and character development.

### GREG (Co-protagonist)
- **Age/Generation:** Gen X, early-mid 50s
- **Physical:** Leaner, shorter. Dark hair, angular features, sharp jawline, intense eyes, trimmed beard/stubble
- **Background:** Creative collaborator with audio/narration experience; semi-anonymity preference
- **Psychology:** Competitive. Vision-driven. Masks vulnerability with structure and challenge. Creatively ambitious, psychologically complex
- **First accidental Run:** "Deadlands" — Western Horror/Supernatural prototype with Pirates of the Caribbean narrative structure (traumatic experience)
- **Revised approach:** Crew feedback led to "The Run Game - Deadlands (Revised)" — less descriptors, more story, Stephen King-like approach using male narrator
- **Arc Invariant:** Greg's growth is acceptance, not victory. Trauma is integrated, never erased.
- **Fading medallion tattoo:** Permanent visual marker, must be visible in Deadlands Run sequences and consistent across all visual media
- **Narrative role:** Secondary POV sections; represents the player who experiences unexpected emotional difficulty when swapping Runs

### JASON (Secondary Player)
- Observer-participant
- Narrative triangulation role — calls out patterns Gene and Greg miss
- Active participant in The Game's callback system
- Does not dominate Runs
- Less developed than Gene/Greg; serves narrative function of external observation

### TRAVIS (Judge/Arbiter)
- "Basement Court" judge establishing foundational rules
- Philosophical ballast
- Increasingly tested as systems collide
- Never omniscient
- Rule enforcement consistency across all Runs; maintains meta-narrative credibility

### NPC Consistency — Deadlands Run
- **Jessie:** Sarcastic saloon girl; recurring character establishing emotional anchors
- **Bob the Bartender:** Gruff, sympathetic; provides moral grounding and recurring relationship continuity

## Visual Continuity — Critical for All AI Generation

**Faces override style. Always.**

### Three-Style System

| Mode | Color Palette | Line Work | Lighting | Composition |
|------|--------------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Horror/Trauma** | Dark, desaturated, gritty | Harsh, angular, sharp | Shadow-heavy, high contrast | Claustrophobic, distorted perspective |
| **Wholesome/Happy** | Pastel, warm | Soft, rounded, gentle curves | Golden hour, warm ambient | Open framing, expansive |
| **Neutral Deck** | Natural, realistic | Balanced angular/curved | Natural, photorealistic | Grounded, naturalistic |

**Absolute Continuity Requirement:** Character faces MUST remain immediately recognizable across all three styles. If a reader can't identify Gene or Greg instantly, the image is invalid.

- Gene's rectangular glasses, fuller face, and kind eyes must be identifiable in dark, gritty Deadlands scenes
- Greg's sharp jawline and intense eyes must remain recognizable in pastel Wholesome runs

## Narrative Structure

### Story Arc Framework
1. **Inciting Incident:** Discovery of Magic Tome at farmhouse; accidental entry into first Runs
2. **Twin Narrative Introduction:** Gene's positive medieval experience vs. Greg's traumatic Deadlands experience
3. **Swap Arc:** Gene enters Deadlands (less traumatic due to foreknowledge); Greg enters medieval world (encounters residual trauma, less positive outcome)
4. **Escalation Phase:** Competitive Run design; callback system introduction; The Game meta-layer development
5. **Mid-series Arcs:** Multiple Runs designed by Gene and Greg; increasing complexity; deeper callbacks
6. **Character Development Threads:**
   - Gene's emotional journey from cautious observer to confident Run architect
   - Greg's processing of trauma; competitive drive masking psychological vulnerability
   - Jason's role evolution from observer to participant
   - Travis's arbitration becoming increasingly tested
7. **Finale Setup:** Crossover event planning; both universes' narratives reaching convergence points

### Webcomic Presentation Structure
- "The Deck" (normal reality setup)
- Run sequence (main narrative)
- Return to "The Deck" (post-run discussion)
- Alternate perspective sequence (Jason's/Travis's viewpoint on same Run)
- Merchandise integration: End-of-chapter in-story scene featuring character-specific merchandise tied to chapter events

1. Production Bible creation and maintenance
2. Character stock sheets development (reference images for consistent rendering)
3. Run outlining (narrative structure, pacing, major story beats)
4. Story generation (dialogue, internal monologue, character actions)
5. Panel breaking (visual composition planning; panel flow design)
6. Illustration generation (AI-assisted or manual; must match style guide)

Reference images generated for AI to use across Runs. Establishes visual baseline for consistency management. Includes character base states, clothing variations, emotional states, and environmental anchors.

## Merchandise Integration Strategy

### Narrative-Embedded Monetization
- **End-of-chapter format:** In-story scene presenting character-specific merchandise tied to that chapter's events
- **Psychological mechanism:** High reader engagement through narrative integration rather than jarring commercial breaks
- **Product design:** Items must feel organically connected to Run experiences
- **Revenue model:** Direct sales; audience investment in character emotional journey increases purchase likelihood
