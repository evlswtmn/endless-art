# The DLC Heist Movie

**Status:** `idea`
**Type:** film
**One-liner:** Game devs discover their corporate overlords will gut their game's soul — so they hide the real story as a secret DLC.

## The Concept

A heist movie set in the world of video game development. A team of developers discover
that their corporate overlords are going to cut the "soul" of the game (the single-player
campaign) to sell the assets as NFTs or battle passes. The devs secretly encode the real
story into the game's code as a hidden DLC — an act of digital rebellion.

## Key Elements

- Heist movie structure applied to game development
- "The score" is preserving art, not stealing money
- Corporate vs. creative tension as the central conflict
- The "heist crew" is a team of devs with different specialties
- Technical details of hiding code could be real and satisfying
- Commentary on the state of the gaming industry (monetization, crunch, creative freedom)
- The hidden DLC reveal as the climactic "vault opening" moment

## Inspiration / References

- Ocean's Eleven (heist crew structure)
- The gaming industry's real controversies (loot boxes, cut content, crunch)
- Indie Game: The Movie (passion vs. industry)
- Mr. Robot (digital rebellion, technical accuracy)
- Silicon Valley (tech industry comedy)

## Open Questions

- Tone — serious thriller or comedy heist?
- Does the heist succeed? What are the consequences?
- How technical do the code-hiding scenes get?
- Is the game fictional or a parody of a real franchise?
- Does the public discover the hidden DLC, or is it a secret?
- Who's the "villain" — a specific exec or the system itself?
