## Multiverse Collision Rules

### First Contact Conditions
- Magic Time manifests inside the Crucible
- This is unprecedented — it has never appeared outside the farmhouse
- The AT system reacts **defensively, not curiously**
- The TRG system does not understand the Crucible's rules

### System Incompatibilities
These are the core tensions that make the crossover work:

| TRG Rule | AT Rule | Collision Effect |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| Exit via completion, failure, or safe-word | Exit only via victory or death | Safe-word may provide escape from AT — or may not |
| Time dilation (subjective ≠ objective) | No time dilation (consistent time) | Both may coexist in collision zones |
| Designer must participate first | Pre-selection via algorithm | Designer-participation rule conflicts with pre-selection |
| Consent-based entry | Forced/coerced participation | Fundamental ethical collision |
| Basement Court meta-awareness | Corporate Entertainment control | Meta-awareness may exceed corporate control |
| Character agency (Runs) | Predetermined narrative (AT) | Agency vs. determinism as central conflict |
| Callbacks from TRG | Callbacks from AT | May nest within each other |

## Crossover Narrative Structure

### Act One: System Collision
- Gene and Greg discover APEX TERMINUS existence
- Realize THE RUN GAME might be nested within a larger structure
- First magical breach: Magic Time manifests in the Crucible
- Gene and Greg make the decision to enter

### Act Two: Contestant Alliance
- Gene and Greg emerge in the arena as "impossible" contestants
- Initial conflict with the AT system
- Alliance formed with Maria
- Basement Court meta-layer attempts to communicate with the AT system
- Ghost's resistance recognizes them as potential leverage
- Damien's crisis point: loyalty to system vs. moral reckoning

### Act Three: System Versus System
- Runs become accessible to APEX TERMINUS contestants
- Exodus begins: contestants using Magic Time to escape
- Corporate Entertainment system escalates counter-measures
- Retribution Arc reaches peak: external timelines invade during system crisis
- Multiple realities colliding simultaneously

### Act Four: Resolution Options

**Option A — Optimistic:**
- Gene, Greg, Maria, Ghost, Tommy, and sympathetic contestants escape into Runs
- Runs provide genuine sanctuary; system overload allows clean separation
- APEX TERMINUS system collapses under multiverse pressure
- Final image: New community forming in stable Run universe; Basement Court expanded to include AT refugees
- Theme: Authentic relationships and consent-based competition can coexist with multiverse chaos

**Option B — Ambiguous/Dark:**
- Escape successful but reveals all systems (Runs, AT, Basement Court) are themselves Runs
- Higher-level system visible but inaccessible
- Gene and Greg must question if their choices matter
- Character relationships remain authentic but tainted with uncertainty
- Final image: Safe but uncertain; acceptance of recursive systems

**Option C — Pyrrhic:**
- Escape succeeds but requires sacrifice (Tommy, Ghost, or Damien dies)
- Victory costs something irreplaceable
- Survivors bear psychological weight of system knowledge
- Basement Court and refugee communities form, but shadow of manipulation remains
- Final image: Survival achieved; innocence lost

## Visual Collision Rules

When universes intersect, both aesthetics coexist in tension — they do not blend cleanly.

### TRG Characters in AT Space
- Gene and Greg must remain visually recognizable despite transitioning into AT visual style (post-apocalyptic grittiness)
- Gene's rectangular glasses, kind eyes, fuller face — non-negotiable even in Crucible ash tones
- Greg's sharp jawline, intense eyes, fading medallion tattoo — must read clearly against industrial brutalism

### AT Characters in TRG Space
- Maria, Damien, Tommy, Ghost must become recognizable within Run aesthetics
- Sponsor-laden jumpsuits slowly replaced by Run-authentic clothing
- Visual metaphor: external system markers (sponsor gear) gradually give way to Run authenticity markers

### System Aesthetic Collision
- Commentary booth aesthetic (corporate sleek) attempting to narrate Run mechanics
- Synthia Volt encountering Basement Court meta-awareness — visual confusion
- Corporate sleekness meeting farmhouse aesthetic creates deliberate visual dissonance
- Authority structures competing for narrative control

## Interaction Rules (Crossover-Specific)

1. Magic Time can manifest in external locations (the Crucible)
2. Runs become accessible to non-TRG characters
3. Time dilation and consistent time may coexist in collision zones
4. Designer-participation rule may conflict with pre-selection algorithm
5. Basement Court meta-awareness may exceed Corporate Entertainment control
6. Character agency (Runs) collides with predetermined narrative (AT)
7. Safe-word mechanism may provide escape from AT
8. Callbacks from both systems may nest within each other

## Character Continuity Non-Negotiables

### Gene's Arc Must Remain Consistent
- Kind eyes and empathy unchanged through crossover
- Growth from cautious to confident maintained
- Moral compass intact — he does not become a Crucible contestant in mentality

### Greg's Arc Must Remain Consistent
- Acceptance over victory trajectory unbroken
- Trauma integrated, never weaponized for AT entertainment
- Fading medallion tattoo visible in all crossover scenes

### Maria's Arc Must Remain Consistent
- Resistance without naïveté
- Nursing instinct preserved even in Run environments
- Does not become subordinate to TRG characters — she leads in her domain

### Damien's Arc Must Remain Consistent
- Redemption possible, not guaranteed — crossover does not force his hand
- Moral erosion visible in behavior before dialogue
- System loyalty vs. moral reckoning remains genuinely unresolved until it isn't
